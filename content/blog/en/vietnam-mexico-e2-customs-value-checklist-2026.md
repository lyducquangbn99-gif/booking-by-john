For a shipment from Vietnam to Mexico, the electronic E2 `Manifestación de Valor` is the Mexican importer's filing, not a form submitted by the Vietnamese exporter. But the importer cannot prepare a coherent customs-value record without accurate commercial, payment and transport data from the supplier and logistics chain.

Mexico's 2026 Foreign Trade General Rules require Form E2 to be transmitted through VUCEM for each foreign-trade operation, subject to specified exceptions. The transition that allowed the previous procedure ended on 31 March 2026, and VUCEM's current FAQ states that the old customs-value worksheet ceased to apply from 1 April 2026 when electronic transmission became compulsory.

The filing must occur before payment of duties and, where applicable, countervailing duties shown in the customs declaration. VUCEM generates a 13-character folio that is declared in record 507 of the pedimento as an e-document. This makes value-file readiness an upstream customs milestone, even though it is not a carrier booking document.

## Seven checks before cargo leaves Vietnam

### 1. Confirm the Mexican importer and the E2 owner

Record the Mexican importer's legal name and RFC, the customs broker or agency, the planned customs office and the person responsible for VUCEM transmission. VUCEM says the party introducing goods into Mexico is responsible for transmitting the E2 using its e.firma or VUCEM digital-seal certificates.

The exporter should provide evidence, not sign or transmit the importer's declaration. Ask the importer or broker whether the operation falls within a stated exception, such as a registered simplified courier operation or an applicable authorised-operator facility. Do not infer an exception from shipment size, mode or Incoterm.

### 2. Freeze one commercial data set

Align the purchase order, contract, commercial invoice or equivalent document, packing list and shipping instruction. Use the same seller, buyer, product description, quantity, currency, unit price, total price and payment terms throughout.

Mexico's Customs Law defines the transaction price as the total payment already made or to be made, directly or indirectly, to or for the benefit of the seller. VUCEM separately asks for price paid and price payable. A deposit, balance payment, credit, compensation arrangement or consignment transaction therefore needs a traceable explanation rather than a nominal value.

### 3. State the Incoterm and split transport costs by leg

Write the agreed Incoterm and named place exactly. Separate origin handling, international freight, insurance, loading and unloading, and post-import inland costs on the invoice or supporting schedule where relevant.

Under Article 65 of the Customs Law, specified transport, insurance, handling, loading and unloading costs can be additions when borne by the importer and not already included in the price. Article 66 excludes specified post-import transport and related costs only when they are separately distinguished. The exporter should not decide the Mexican customs value, but it should give the importer enough detail to classify each cost correctly.

### 4. Disclose assists, royalties and related-party facts

Ask whether the Mexican buyer supplied moulds, tooling, components, designs or other production inputs free of charge or at a reduced price. Identify any royalty or licence payment connected with the goods and any proceeds that later revert to the seller.

Also disclose whether buyer and seller are related. Form E2 asks about that relationship, and Article 67 says transaction value can be used when no relationship exists or when the relationship did not influence the value. Do not hide a relationship or assume it automatically invalidates transaction value; give the importer and customs adviser the facts needed to select and support the method.

### 5. Assemble evidence before the E2 cut-off

Provide the final invoice or equivalent document, contract, bill of lading or airway bill, payment evidence available at the time, and schedules supporting freight, insurance, assists, royalties, additions and separately stated deductions.

VUCEM's FAQ says the supporting file should substantiate the declared value and valuation method, and cites the equivalent document and bill of lading among the examples. Mexico's Customs Law also lists invoices, transfers or letters of credit, transport and insurance costs, contracts and evidence for Article 65 additions and Article 66 exclusions in the importer's electronic customs file.

### 6. Reconcile COVE, pedimento and partial shipments

Before the importer submits E2, compare the invoice data, COVE, transport document and draft pedimento. VUCEM allows one E2 to include one or more COVE acknowledgements. If a COVE is subdivided across declarations, only the amount corresponding to that subdivision should be used so it remains coherent with the pedimento.

For multiple Incoterms in one operation, the FAQ says the most important in monetary terms is declared and the other terms are explained in the upload area. Treat this as a documentation task, not permission to blend costs without a reconciliation.

### 7. Keep valuation separate from origin and product compliance

The E2 value file does not replace tariff classification, CPTPP origin support, import permits, sector registrations, NOM requirements, sanitary controls, quotas or trade-remedy checks. Run those gates in parallel.

If E2 information or attachments are incomplete or inaccurate, Rule 1.5.1 requires a new form in VUCEM; when the declared customs value is affected, the pedimento must be rectified where applicable. VUCEM says spontaneous correction may be possible under the tax code, but this is not a guarantee that every error can be corrected at any time or without consequence.

## What this means for Vietnam freight and export operations

The operational risk is a data mismatch discovered after cargo has departed: an invoice that bundles freight, an unexplained deposit, a free mould omitted from the value file, or a different total between COVE and the pedimento. Those problems can stop the importer from completing a coherent pre-payment filing even though the carrier has accepted the cargo.

For recurring Mexico traffic, add five fields to the shipment release gate: `E2 owner`, `valuation pack complete`, `Incoterm cost split`, `COVE/pedimento reconciled` and `separate origin/product-control status`. Release the booking only when open items have a named owner and documented resolution path.

Confidence is high in the 1 April 2026 operational date, per-operation transmission, pre-payment timing, 13-character folio and required value concepts because they come from SAT, VUCEM, the Diario Oficial and Mexico's current Customs Law. Confidence is lower for the treatment of a particular shipment. The importer and its licensed customs adviser must determine the valuation method, applicable additions or exclusions, exceptions and correction route from the actual transaction. No source used here guarantees carrier acceptance, customs release, a clearance time, inspection outcome, duty amount, storage cost or freedom from penalties.
