Current-news label: verified against official Vietnamese and Singapore government sources on 26 October 2026. The figures are trade values, not container, air-cargo, weight or shipment counts.

Vietnam's Ministry of Industry and Trade reported on 21 October that Vietnam–Singapore merchandise trade reached nearly SGD 42.8 billion in the first nine months of 2026, up 46.8% year on year. Singapore imported SGD 19.4 billion from Vietnam, up 123.2%, while exporting SGD 23.4 billion to Vietnam, up 14.3%.

The concentration is the useful freight signal. Singapore's imports of electrical machinery, equipment and parts under HS 85 from Vietnam reached nearly SGD 9.54 billion, up 118.8%, and represented 49.2% of Singapore's total imports from Vietnam in the period.

## What the new data do and do not say

Enterprise Singapore's 16 October release provides the wider setting: Singapore's total merchandise trade rose 50.8% year on year in September, non-oil domestic exports rose 68.4%, and non-oil re-exports rose 47.8%. It says electronics continued to surge, supported by AI-related demand and driven mainly by disk media products, integrated circuits and personal computers.

But none of these releases publishes Vietnam–Singapore TEU, air-cargo tonnes, chargeable weight, shipment count, freight rate, capacity, routing or lead time. Singapore's Department of Statistics explains that merchandise imports are recorded at CIF value and that transshipment cargo is excluded from merchandise trade statistics. The Ministry also notes that much of the Vietnamese cargo entering Singapore continues onward to third countries.

The practical conclusion is therefore narrower than “freight volume doubled.” The value signal is strong and concentrated, but each booking still needs physical and commercial evidence.

## Six controls before turning the signal into a freight plan

### 1. Separate HS 85 from the actual product

Record the six-digit HS code, product description, part number and intended use. A chapter-level value cannot determine packing method, dangerous-goods status, security handling or customs treatment for a particular shipment.

### 2. Identify Singapore's role

Confirm whether the buyer will consume, manufacture with, store, consolidate or re-export the goods. Record the final destination when known. This prevents a Singapore sales value from being treated automatically as final Singapore demand.

### 3. Build capacity from physical order data

Use booked units, gross and net weight, dimensions, pallet or carton count, stackability and required delivery window. Then compare sea, air or multimodal options. Do not convert SGD growth directly into TEU or airfreight demand.

### 4. Verify the actual routing

Ask the carrier or forwarder for the named origin, port or airport pair, direct or transshipment plan, cut-offs and current schedule. The official trade releases do not identify the transport mode or route used by the reported goods.

### 5. Align commercial values and documents

Reconcile invoice value, currency, Incoterm, insurance, freight, customs declaration and country-of-origin evidence. Singapore's import statistics use CIF values, so a statistical import value is not automatically the exporter's FOB revenue or the shipment's freight charge.

### 6. Keep a dated evidence pack

Save the purchase order, packing list, product classification, origin documents, routing confirmation and booking quotation used for the decision. Recheck them when the order, mode, route or final destination changes.

## Vietnam freight and import-export impact

The data support closer attention to Vietnam–Singapore electronics and machinery flows, especially where Singapore functions as a distribution or re-export node. Exporters and forwarders can use the signal to ask earlier for order forecasts and physical cargo data, reserve only against confirmed requirements, and separate Singapore destination demand from onward movement.

Confidence is high in the published values, growth rates and statistical definitions. Confidence is low for any conclusion about physical freight growth because the cited sources do not provide Vietnam-specific volume, mode, routing, capacity, rate or transit-time data. This package makes no forecast and promises no space, rate, clearance or delivery outcome.
