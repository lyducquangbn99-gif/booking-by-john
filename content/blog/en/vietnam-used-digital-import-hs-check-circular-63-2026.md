Vietnam's Ministry of Science and Technology issued Circular 63/2026/TT-BKHCN on 27 October 2026. It was published in Electronic Official Gazette No. 572 on 6 November and takes effect on 1 January 2027. The issue date, Gazette publication date and effective date are separate.

The circular publishes the detailed HS-coded list of used digital-technology products prohibited from import under Decree 292/2026/ND-CP. It replaces Circular 11/2018/TT-BTTTT from the effective date, subject to a transition for goods already authorised by a competent authority before 1 January 2027.

This is a prohibition list, not a general licensing guide for all electronics. Shipment treatment depends on the goods' actual condition, function, components and customs classification, plus any valid permission or specialised pathway. A commercial description such as “refurbished,” “tested” or “like new” does not by itself take a product outside the list.

## How the HS list must be read

Circular 63 sets three levels of application. If the annex identifies a four-digit HS heading, the prohibition applies to every eight-digit code under that heading. If it identifies a six-digit subheading, it applies to every eight-digit code under that subheading. If it lists an eight-digit code, only that code is covered.

The annex includes, among other entries, printers, copiers and fax equipment under heading 8443 with stated exclusions; calculating and data-processing equipment under headings 8470 and 8471; phones and network equipment under 8517; audio equipment under 8518; transmission equipment and digital cameras under 8525; radar, radio-navigation and remote-control apparatus under 8526; radio receivers under 8527; monitors, projectors and television reception equipment under 8528; printed circuits under 8534; electronic tubes under 8540; integrated circuits under 8542; and specified insulated-wire, cable and optical-fibre lines under 8544.

The annex must be read exactly. For example, the notes under heading 8443 exclude multicolour digital printers and copiers, and also exclude certain used monochrome digital printers or copiers capable of A0 printing or at least 35 A4 pages per minute. The annex also excludes laser toner cartridges from the listed inked-cartridge line. These notes are product-specific; they are not a blanket exemption for all printers or consumables.

If an HS code in the circular is inconsistent with Vietnam's official export-import goods nomenclature, Circular 63 says the customs-law rules apply. Importers should therefore avoid treating a supplier's invoice code as final classification evidence.

## Used parts and refurbished products are expressly covered

The prohibition also applies to used parts, accessories, assemblies and electronic components of products on the list. A shipment split into chassis, boards, modules, cables or other used components is not automatically outside the rule.

Circular 63 also states that refurbished digital-technology products whose HS codes fall within the annex are prohibited. For this rule, a refurbished product is a previously used product repaired or fitted with replacement components to restore its function, quality and appearance to an equivalent-to-new condition, while meeting the technical, warranty and labelling requirements applied to new products. Refurbishment therefore does not itself convert listed used goods into permissible new goods.

The circular does preserve a distinct route for used technology lines, machinery, equipment and tools that satisfy the legal criteria for permitted import and directly serve an investment project for semiconductor production, packaging or testing, or digital-technology training, research and development. That provision is narrow and evidence-dependent; it is not a general exception for resale inventory or ordinary office equipment.

## Transition for existing permissions

Circular 11/2018/TT-BTTTT ceases to have effect when Circular 63 takes effect. However, if a competent authority granted permission before 1 January 2027 to import goods on the former prohibited-used-IT list, the import may continue according to the content and validity period of that permission.

The transition does not say that a purchase order, sales contract, booking confirmation or shipment departure alone is equivalent to official permission. The importer should retain the authorising document, verify its validity dates and match the permitted goods, quantity and conditions to the shipment.

## Six controls before accepting the cargo

- Establish whether every unit is new, used, repaired, remanufactured or refurbished, and retain serial-number, model, manufacture-year, repair and component-replacement evidence.
- Classify each finished product at the candidate eight-digit HS code, then check whether a four-digit heading or six-digit subheading in the annex captures the entire code family.
- Screen used parts, accessories, assemblies and electronic components separately; do not rely only on the finished-product name shown on the invoice.
- Read every annex note and exclusion against the technical specification, especially for printers, copiers, cartridges and the specified cable lines.
- If relying on a pre-effective-date permission or the investment/training/R&D machinery route, obtain importer and qualified-adviser confirmation before pickup and match the evidence to the exact cargo.
- Put a booking hold on ambiguous used or refurbished goods until the importer and customs representative confirm condition, HS treatment and the documentary basis in writing.

## Vietnam freight and import-export impact

The main freight effect is an earlier compliance gate. Condition and HS screening should happen before pickup, consolidation or container loading, not after arrival in Vietnam. A mixed lot is especially exposed because one listed used component, an incorrect four-digit-to-eight-digit reading or an unsupported “refurbished” description can interrupt the whole shipment's document review and handling plan.

Forwarders should request model lists, serial numbers, technical sheets, photographs and condition declarations early enough for the importer to classify the cargo. They should also separate clearly permissible new goods from uncertain used goods where commercially and operationally feasible. The circular does not publish a standard inspection rate, clearance time, storage period, penalty, freight charge or guaranteed outcome.

Confidence is high on the instrument number, legal dates, HS application rules, treatment of used components and refurbished products, transition, and the narrow machinery pathway because these points are stated in the official Government record, signed circular and official annex. Confidence is medium on shipment-level timing and cost effects because the official sources do not quantify inspections, holds, port dwell or expenses. This is practical editorial guidance, not a customs classification ruling or legal opinion.
