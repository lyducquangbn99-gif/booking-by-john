Vietnam began Monday, 12 October, with several official weather signals that matter to cargo planning. They do not establish a nationwide port shutdown. They do justify a fresh check of the actual road, barge, feeder, terminal and vessel used by each shipment.

At 04:00 on 12 October, the National Center for Hydro-Meteorological Forecasting warned of showers and thunderstorms across waters from southern Quang Tri to Ca Mau, from Ca Mau to An Giang, the Gulf of Thailand and parts of the East Sea. Thunderstorms could bring tornadoes, force 7-8 gusts and waves above 2 metres. The warning says all vessels operating in the affected areas face possible impact.

The coastal risk is joined by high water in the south. At Vung Tau station, the highest water level reached 4.18 metres at 01:30 on 11 October. NCHMF expected the coastal water level to remain high but ease slightly, with a possible Vung Tau maximum of 4.05-4.15 metres over the next 24-48 hours. Its impact notice identifies possible flooding in low-lying coastal, riverside and outside-dyke areas, slower river drainage, effects on traffic and economic activity, and strong seaward currents as the tide recedes.

NCHMF also reported the Dong Nai River at Ta Lai moving slowly around alert levels 1-2. Its 11 October warning identified flood risk in low-lying riverside areas, possible effects on inland waterway transport and a level-1 natural-hazard risk.

## What was observed in Central Vietnam

The national disaster-management duty report recorded 200-350 millimetres of rain from Ha Tinh to Quang Ngai during the three days ending at 07:00 on 11 October, with isolated totals above 450 millimetres. The same report documented road impacts on 10 October.

In Ha Tinh, five landslide points affected about 250 metres of a coastal defence patrol road, while 920 metres of a village road had flooded and later cleared. In Quang Tri, national roads 9B and 9C had reopened by the report, but three points on provincial road 564 remained under 0.4-0.6 metres of water. These are localized observations, not evidence that every Central Vietnam trucking route is blocked.

## Five checks before the next cargo movement

- Revalidate the exact inland route. Ask the trucker or depot to confirm the planned road, bridge and low-lying access segment close to dispatch time. Do not extrapolate a provincial warning into a blanket route closure.
- Reconfirm the operating window. Compare pickup, empty return, terminal cut-off and gate appointment with the latest tide, rain and road-access update. Obtain the terminal or carrier's own confirmation before changing a booking.
- Check every water leg. For barge, coastal feeder, tug or small-vessel movements, request the latest operating advice and revised estimated times. An official weather warning is not itself a sailing-cancellation notice.
- Protect cargo and documents. Inspect container seals, door gaskets, tarpaulins and floor condition; keep customs and origin documents in a dry digital handoff; verify reefer fuel, power and monitoring responsibility during any hold.
- Pre-authorise the contingency. Record who can approve a different pickup time, safe staging point, barge connection or sailing, together with cost and delivery limits. Update the consignee with verified events rather than forecasts presented as facts.

## Freight and import-export impact

The immediate commercial risk sits at the interfaces: factory-to-depot access, barge and feeder connections, terminal cut-offs, moisture-sensitive cargo and reefer continuity. A short disruption at one interface can cause a missed handoff even when the main international vessel remains on schedule.

No official source in this package announces a closure of Cat Lai, Cai Mep-Thi Vai, Da Nang or another international terminal. None establishes a standard delay, freight-rate increase, equipment shortage, cargo-damage probability or customs suspension. Live terminal, carrier, depot and road confirmation remains necessary.

Confidence is high for the official observed rainfall, tide level and published warnings. Confidence is medium for the direction of operational risk because the warnings cover broad areas but individual routes and facilities differ. Shipment-level confidence is low until the actual inland route, gate, barge, feeder and sailing are confirmed. The weather is time-sensitive; users should replace this snapshot with later official bulletins as they are issued.
