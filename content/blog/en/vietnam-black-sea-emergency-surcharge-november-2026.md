Maersk announced on 13 November 2026 that it will apply Emergency Operational Cost Export (OCE) and Emergency Operational Cost Recovery (OCR) charges on container movements involving Bulgaria, Georgia and Romania. The carrier gives Vietnam a specific effective date of 29 November 2026, until further notice.

The published levels are USD 30 for a 20-foot container and USD 60 for a 40- or 45-foot container, equivalent to USD 30 per TEU. The notice covers OCE from Bulgaria, Georgia and Romania to the world; OCR from the world to Bulgaria and Georgia; and OCR from the world, excluding China, to Romania.

This is a Maersk carrier charge, not a Vietnamese customs tax and not a universal market tariff. The notice does not say that every contract, quotation or booking type will be treated identically.

## Why this matters for Vietnam cargo now

The Joint War Committee's circular JWLA-035, dated 16 September, amended its listed area to include the Black Sea while excluding territorial waters of adjoining countries other than Russia and Ukraine. The circular says application to individual insurance contracts is subject to specific negotiation. Maersk cites the designation as the reason for additional war-risk and voyage costs; the JWC circular itself does not set Maersk's surcharge.

Vietnam Customs' preliminary April dataset confirms that this is a real cargo corridor. It records USD 114.63 million of Vietnamese exports to Romania and USD 36.42 million to Bulgaria in January–April 2026. The Romania total includes seafood, coffee, fertilisers, textiles, glass, iron and steel products, computers and electrical products, phones and other goods. The statistics describe trade value, not Maersk-carried volume and not the number of chargeable containers.

## Six checks before costing or booking

### 1. Match the exact direction and country

For Vietnam-origin cargo, the published inbound OCR scope covers Bulgaria and Georgia from the world and Romania from the world excluding China. For cargo moving from Bulgaria, Georgia or Romania to Vietnam, the published OCE scope is the reverse. Do not copy an import code onto an export movement or treat all Black Sea destinations as listed.

### 2. Obtain the effective-date test in writing

The public notice says Vietnam is effective from 29 November, but it does not state in the effective-date list whether application turns on booking, price calculation, first water-leg departure, gate-in, loading or another event. For cargo straddling that date, ask Maersk to confirm the shipment-specific trigger and keep the reply with the quotation.

### 3. Reconcile equipment size and arithmetic

Check the booking and equipment code. The notice publishes USD 30 for all 20-foot units and USD 60 for all 40- and 45-foot units. For several containers, calculate by actual unit count and size; do not multiply the USD 30-per-TEU shorthand without reconciling it to the carrier's line items.

### 4. Record booking type and price calculation date

For non-SPOT bookings, Maersk says the charge is retrieved by Price Calculation Date. For non-FMC cargo, that is the scheduled departure date of the first water leg at booking confirmation; for FMC cargo, it is the last container gate-in date. The notice does not publish a SPOT-booking rule, so confirm it rather than assuming inclusion or exemption.

### 5. Separate every cost line

The notice says the charge follows the freight-paid location and that other local or contingency surcharges may apply. Keep OCE or OCR separate from ocean freight, terminal charges, inland costs, insurance, customs duties and any other security-related charge. The JWC listed-area circular is an insurance-risk notification; it is not an invoice or a fixed insurance premium.

### 6. Preserve the commercial evidence

Save the booking confirmation, quotation version, Price Calculation Date, equipment list, freight-paid term, carrier confirmation of the 29 November trigger and final invoice. If a charge is disputed, compare the invoice with the route direction, equipment and pricing record rather than relying on the article headline.

## Concrete impact on Vietnam freight and trade

Vietnam exporters to Romania, Bulgaria and Georgia, and importers sourcing from those markets, should add the published charge as a conditional Maersk cost line and confirm it before finalising landed cost or Incoterm responsibility. The amount is modest per container, but inconsistent effective-date or payer assumptions can still create quotation gaps across multi-container shipments.

Confidence is high on the carrier's publication date, Vietnam date, geographic scope, container amounts and non-SPOT PCD definitions because they appear in Maersk's official notice. Confidence is high on the wording and date of JWLA-035 and on the preliminary customs values because they come from the issuing bodies. Confidence is medium at shipment level: the public notice does not define the Vietnam effective-date event or SPOT treatment, and contracts, filed tariffs, quotations or later updates may control. This article is operational guidance, not a rate guarantee, insurance opinion, customs advice or legal advice.
