FCL and LCL are two ways to move cargo by sea from Vietnam. FCL, or Full Container Load, gives one shipper the use of a container. LCL, or Less than Container Load, combines several shippers' cargo in one container. The better option is not decided by volume alone: total origin and destination charges, cargo characteristics, handling risk, schedule and delivery scope all matter.

## What changes when you choose FCL?

With FCL, the container is assigned to one shipper. The cargo may be loaded at a factory or warehouse, or delivered to a container yard depending on the booking and service scope. Common equipment includes 20-foot and 40-foot dry containers, but equipment suitability must be checked for the actual cargo.

FCL can be attractive when the shipment uses a meaningful share of a container, when individual packages are difficult to consolidate, or when fewer handling stages are preferred. It does not mean the container is guaranteed to sail faster, and it does not remove port, customs, trucking, documentation or destination charges.

## What changes when you choose LCL?

With LCL, cargo is delivered to a consolidation warehouse, grouped with other shipments, loaded into a shared container and separated again at destination. Charges are commonly based on chargeable weight or measurement, subject to minimums and local tariffs.

LCL can suit smaller shipments that do not justify a full container. However, the quote should be checked beyond the ocean-freight line. Consolidation, deconsolidation, warehouse handling, documentation, pickup and destination fees can materially affect the total.

## Compare these six decision points

- **Volume and weight:** Ask for both FCL and LCL totals when cargo is approaching a practical break-even range. There is no universal CBM threshold because rates, density and local charges vary by route and date.
- **Total landed logistics cost:** Compare every known origin, ocean and destination charge—not only the base freight.
- **Handling risk:** LCL normally involves more warehouse and cargo-handling stages. Packaging should be suitable for shared-container movement and stacking conditions.
- **Timing:** LCL needs consolidation before departure and deconsolidation after arrival. Actual schedules and cut-offs must be confirmed for the booking.
- **Cargo compatibility:** Dangerous goods, controlled goods, fragile pieces, liquids, food products and oversized cargo may face special acceptance or segregation rules.
- **Delivery scope:** Include pickup, export formalities, destination clearance and final delivery in the same comparison where possible.

## A simple working rule

Choose LCL for a smaller, well-packed shipment when its complete door-to-door cost is lower and the extra handling stages are acceptable. Choose FCL when the all-in difference becomes small, the cargo fills enough of the container, or control and reduced handling matter more than the lowest base rate.

The final choice should come from two comparable quotations built on the same route, cargo details, Incoterm and delivery scope. A low LCL ocean rate is not automatically the cheapest complete solution; an FCL rate is not automatically better simply because the cargo is bulky.
