For Vietnam-origin ocean cargo sold to Italy, the Entry Summary Declaration is not a form to reconstruct after the container reaches Europe. ICS2 uses ENS data for safety and security risk analysis before entry, and the usable information begins with the exporter, buyer, seller, freight forwarder and transport documents.

The European Commission’s ICS2 page says goods brought into or transiting through the EU must be covered by safety and security data, subject to the legal scope and exemptions. Italy’s Customs and Monopolies Agency confirms that maritime Release 3 is in operation and that operators submit ENS data to the EU central system.

For ordinary containerised cargo moving from Vietnam by sea, Article 105 of the current consolidated EU delegated regulation sets the legal ENS deadline at the latest 24 hours before the goods are loaded onto the vessel on which they will enter the EU customs territory. This is the legal outer deadline, not a promise that a carrier will accept shipping instructions that late. A carrier or filing party may impose an earlier documentary cut-off.

That distinction matters now. Maersk’s advisory dated 7 September introduced a carrier-specific “No MRN, No Load” policy from 30 September 2026 for cargo requiring ICS2 and moving to or through the EU. Maersk says a valid Movement Reference Number must be received before loading and that cargo without one at least 24 hours before vessel arrival at the applicable compliance load port may be excluded from the load list and rolled. This is Maersk’s operating policy, not a new EU-wide statutory deadline for every carrier.

## Seven checks before confirming the loading plan

### 1. Identify the first EU entry point

Record the vessel that will bring the goods into the EU and its first EU port. The first customs office of entry may be outside Italy even when the final discharge or delivery is in Italy. Do not build the ENS plan only from the Italian destination or the first booking leg from Vietnam.

### 2. Fix the filing model and owners in writing

Confirm whether the carrier will make one complete ENS filing or whether the shipment will use multiple partial filings. If information is split, name the carrier, house-level filer and party monitoring responses. The Commission’s FAQ says the parties need a contractual arrangement and must exchange the linking data in advance, including relevant EORI numbers and the transport-contract reference.

### 3. Freeze the master, house and goods-item structure

Map every master bill, lowest house bill and goods item before sending shipping instructions. The Commission’s March 2026 key-requirements factsheet says accurate and complete data are required at master, lowest-house and goods-item levels. Do not collapse several underlying shipments into one generic house entry.

### 4. Separate every six-digit HS line

Provide a valid six-digit Harmonized System code for each commodity and separate items with different HS6 codes. Treat the code as ENS data, not as a substitute for the Italian import classification decision. The Italian importer and customs representative should still confirm the classification used for the import declaration and any product controls.

### 5. Use real parties and meaningful descriptions

At the lowest house level, provide the actual consignor and consignee and the required buyer, seller and address fields. Avoid descriptions such as “accessories,” “apparel,” “auto,” “unknown” or “not available” without a precise commodity description. Reconcile names, addresses, postal codes and countries against the commercial invoice, packing list and house bill.

### 6. Build time for rejection, amendment and referral

Submit before the carrier’s stated documentation cut-off, not at the legal last minute. Track whether the filing was accepted and whether an MRN was issued. Correct and resubmit messages returned with errors. If customs requests more information or an amendment, assign an owner and respond before treating the shipment as load-ready.

### 7. Keep ENS, loading and Italian import clearance separate

An accepted ENS or MRN is a safety-and-security milestone. It is not proof of customs import release, tariff treatment, VAT payment, licence compliance, sanitary or product-control clearance, equipment availability or vessel loading. Keep separate evidence for carrier acceptance, actual loading, arrival notification, presentation of goods and the Italian import declaration.

## Concrete impact for Vietnam exporters and forwarders

The practical risk sits upstream. A vague product name, missing house party, inactive EORI, mismatched transport-document number or unclear first EU port can force correction while the carrier’s load list is closing. A Vietnamese shipper may not lodge the ENS itself, but it controls much of the source data that the carrier, forwarder and Italian importer need.

Add these fields to the shipment control sheet: `first EU port and vessel`, `ENS filing model`, `carrier and house filer EORI`, `master/house references`, `HS6 by item`, `carrier documentation cut-off`, `ENS status and MRN`, and `referral owner`. Revalidate them when routing or documents change.

Confidence is high on the ICS2 scope, filing roles, house-level and HS6 requirements, and the standard 24-hour maritime legal deadline because they come from current EU law and European Commission guidance. Confidence is high on what Italy’s customs authority says about its ICS2 connection and on Maersk’s own policy. Shipment-level confidence is medium because the actual filing model, carrier cut-off, first EU entry point and response to a rejected filing depend on the booked service and parties. This guide does not guarantee loading, transit time, customs release or final delivery and is not legal or customs advice.
