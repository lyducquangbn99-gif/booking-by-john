CMA CGM Vietnam announced on 5 November that import demurrage free time for non-operating reefer containers will be reduced by one day from 1 December 2026. The notice names four carrier brands: CMA CGM, CNC, ANL and APL. It asks customers to plan cargo collection to avoid possible demurrage.

NOR means a reefer container used without refrigeration and not plugged in. This is a carrier operating change, not a change to Vietnamese customs law, and it does not apply automatically to every dry, operating reefer or special container.

The currently published CMA CGM Vietnam tariff, effective 1 January 2026, shows four free calendar days of import NOR demurrage and three free calendar days of NOR detention. Reading that tariff together with the one-day reduction indicates a standard CMA CGM demurrage window of three free days from 1 December. That is editorial arithmetic, not a substitute for the final December tariff, a service contract or shipment-specific free-time terms. The notice covers four brands, so the customer should verify the applicable brand tariff and booking conditions rather than generalise the CMA CGM grid.

## Six checks before 1 December

### 1. Confirm the equipment and carrier brand

Check the equipment code on the booking, bill of lading, arrival notice and terminal record. Confirm that the unit is treated as NOR and identify whether the bill is issued for CMA CGM, CNC, ANL or APL. Do not apply the notice to a standard dry box or an operating reefer merely because the cargo is non-temperature-controlled.

### 2. Ask what makes the new rule applicable

The public notice gives an effective date but does not state on its text page whether application turns on arrival, discharge, bill of lading, booking or another event. Obtain the shipment-specific rule in writing for cargo arriving around 1 December. Do not infer it from the vessel ETA alone.

### 3. Build the countdown from actual discharge

CMA CGM’s general terms define import demurrage as the period from vessel discharge until gate-out of the full container. The published Vietnam tariff says the free days and excess days are calendar days, including weekends and public holidays. The general terms say the first chargeable day follows the last free day and each day or part of a day is due in full. Record the actual discharge timestamp and the carrier-confirmed last free day.

### 4. Make the release file ready before arrival

Reconcile the consignee and notify-party details, bill of lading status, freight-payment confirmation, delivery-order requirements, licences, certificates and customs file. CMA CGM Vietnam’s import guide warns that cross-payment confirmation and required import documents can affect cargo release. Assign an owner and completion time to every unresolved item.

### 5. Reserve pickup and return capacity

Pre-align the customs broker, trucker, terminal appointment, warehouse receiving slot and empty-return depot. Add a fallback truck and escalation contact. A shorter terminal free-time window increases the cost of waiting until release is complete before looking for transport capacity.

### 6. Separate the cost lines and preserve evidence

Keep arrival notice, discharge record, last-free-day confirmation, gate-out record, invoice and dispute evidence in one file. Demurrage, detention, terminal storage and reefer services are not the same charge. CMA CGM’s terms state that storage and reefer services are separate unless a published tariff says otherwise. Request a shipment-specific D&D calculation instead of treating one free-time figure as the entire landed-cost exposure.

## Concrete impact on Vietnam imports

Importers using NOR equipment for dry cargo have less buffer between discharge and terminal pickup under the announced change. A weekend, holiday, document correction, payment confirmation, customs hold or unavailable truck can consume a larger share of the free period. The practical response is to move release checks and pickup planning before arrival, not to assume one extra day can be recovered after discharge.

Confidence is high on the notice date, 1 December effective date, four named brands, one-day reduction, NOR definition and the carrier’s general timing rules because they come from CMA CGM’s official pages. Confidence is medium on the resulting shipment-level free days because contracts, special arrangements, brand tariffs and the carrier’s effective-date test may differ. The three-day figure is explicitly an inference from the currently published CMA CGM tariff and the announced one-day reduction.
