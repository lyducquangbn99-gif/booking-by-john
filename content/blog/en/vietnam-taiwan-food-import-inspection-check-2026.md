For a commercial food shipment from Vietnam to Taiwan, the most useful pre-booking question is not simply whether the cargo is “food.” It is whether the exact Taiwan CCC code carries import regulation F01 and, if it does, whether the importer can submit one internally consistent inspection case.

Taiwan's official import-regulation code list states that food under F01 follows the Regulations of Inspection of Imported Foods and Related Products and that the importer applies to the Taiwan Food and Drug Administration (TFDA). The inspection regulations were amended on 21 August 2025; most provisions took effect on 1 January 2026, while Articles 19 to 21 on prior release and guarantee deposits took effect on 1 September 2025.

This guide covers the TFDA and customs handoff for commercial food and related products. It does not replace product-specific rules on animal or plant quarantine, additives, health certificates, registration, compositional standards or special country controls.

## Six checks before booking

### 1. Confirm the Taiwan CCC code and every attached import code

Ask the Taiwanese importer and broker to confirm the CCC code from the product's ingredients, processing, intended use and presentation—not from the marketing name alone. Record whether F01 applies and whether the same tariff line also carries another approval, inspection or quarantine code. Do this before fixing the sailing, because a later classification change can alter the responsible agency, documents and handling plan.

### 2. Freeze the “same batch” identity

Under Article 5 of the TFDA regulations, products in the same inspection case must share the same import declaration, CCC code, product name, ingredients, brand, producer and country of origin. Build one controlled data sheet containing those fields, plus pack size, lot or production references, quantity and temperature condition where relevant.

Use that sheet to reconcile the Vietnamese commercial invoice, packing list, product specification, artwork or label data, transport instruction and the Taiwanese importer's application. A small spelling or manufacturer mismatch can become an inspection-file correction even when the physical goods have not changed.

### 3. Prepare the TFDA application before arrival

The obligatory inspection applicant must apply to the inspection authority at the port of entry within 15 days before the entry date or upon arrival. The application includes an inspection application form, product information sheet, a copy of the import declaration and any other documents or information required by the authority.

The 15-day window is permission to apply early, not a promised processing time. The Taiwanese importer should name the applicant or representative, confirm system access and agree a document cut-off with the exporter and broker before the carrier's final shipping-instruction cut-off.

### 4. Assemble one customs-ready document pack

Taiwan Customs lists the commercial invoice and packing list among the core import documents. Depending on filing mode and the shipment, Customs may also require the delivery order or AWB, authorization, import permit, catalogue or explanatory material, certificate of origin, other agency documents and a container loading list when more than one container is declared.

The document pack should use the same product description, units, quantities, consignee and origin facts as the TFDA file. Keep the regulatory approval reference separate from commercial purchase-order or booking references so that each number is placed in the correct declaration field.

### 5. Make the logistics inspection-ready

TFDA verification or sampling occurs where the goods are stored. For full-container-load cargo, it is conducted at a centralized inspection area or another area approved by the inspection authority. If inspection is difficult or time-consuming, the authority may require the container to be unpacked and the goods moved into a warehouse. The applicant must cooperate, and the associated handling costs are borne by the applicant.

Before booking, agree who can arrange positioning, lifting, opening, sampling support, cold-chain protection and resealing. Add contingency time, but do not assume that every shipment will be sampled or that a particular inspection method, time or charge will apply. The regulations provide several methods, including batch-by-batch inspection, reinforced random inspection at 20%–50%, regular random inspection at 2%–10%, batch-by-batch verification, certification inspection and monitoring inspection.

### 6. Lock the label and conditional-release plan

TFDA states that packaged food for business use generally needs complete Chinese labeling under Article 22 of the food law. Imported food intended for repacking, subdivision or further processing may omit complete Chinese labeling on the outer package when identifiable original information is available, but otherwise Chinese labeling must be complete before sale. Treat any post-import relabeling plan as a declared and controlled process, not an informal warehouse fix.

Prior release is also conditional, not automatic. For products not subject to batch-by-batch inspection, TFDA may allow prior customs clearance under a safekeeping bond when analysis takes more than five days, verification or sampling is difficult, goods are easily spoiled, or specified ship-loading and dock-storage conditions apply. Some cases require a guarantee deposit equal to one, two or four times the duty-paying value. Confirm eligibility, storage location and financial exposure with the importer; do not build a delivery promise around prior release.

## Freight and trade impact

The practical benefit is a cleaner handoff among the Vietnamese producer, exporter, forwarder, Taiwanese importer, broker and inspection applicant. Locking the CCC code and same-batch identity before loading can reduce avoidable amendments, while an inspection-ready delivery plan prevents the operations team from treating sampling access as an afterthought.

This workflow does not guarantee TFDA acceptance, a specific inspection channel, prior release, customs release time, cold-storage availability or cost. Confidence is high for the rules stated directly in the official TFDA, Customs and trade-administration sources. Confidence is medium for an individual shipment until the Taiwanese importer and broker verify the live CCC code, all attached import regulations and product-specific requirements. This is operational information, not legal advice or a binding classification decision.
