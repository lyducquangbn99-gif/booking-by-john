Vietnamese exporters and forwarders shipping smartphones to Mexico should put product compliance before cargo handover. Mexico's 29 May 2026 amendment added tariff lines 8517.13.01, 8517.14.91 and 8517.18.99 to the relevant NOM treatment for mobile phones and smartphones. A same-day SNICE notice postponed applicability of the NOM-001-SCFI-2018 and NOM-024-SCFI-2013 changes until 1 July 2026.

This is a product-compliance change, not a carrier surcharge, port restriction or ban on Vietnam-origin goods. Its practical effect is that the Mexican importer and customs representative need a shipment file that matches the exact product, tariff classification and import procedure before departure.

## What changed on 1 July

The amendment subjects the three listed tariff lines to NOM-001-SCFI-2018 at the point of entry. Mexico's official standards platform describes NOM-001 as the current safety standard for electronic equipment. The amendment also updates the scope notes for those same lines under NOM-024-SCFI-2013, the current commercial-information standard for packaging, instructions and warranties of electronic, electrical and household products.

The instrument published in the Official Gazette says it takes effect the day after publication. SNICE then postponed only the applicability of the NOM-001 and NOM-024 changes until 1 July 2026. For a shipment crossing that cutover, ask the Mexican customs representative to confirm the applicable treatment in writing.

The amendment also names the three lines among goods that may not use the cited exemptions in rule 2.4.11 IX and IXBIS for simplified courier imports up to USD 2,500 or non-commercial goods below USD 1,000. Low value or courier routing therefore should not be assumed to remove the NOM check.

## Six checks before accepting a booking

- Classify each model with the Mexican importer and customs representative. Do not apply one tariff line to an entire product family without checking its technical description.
- Confirm the NOM-001 compliance route for the exact model and planned import regime. Record which certificate or electronic document the importer will transmit with the customs entry.
- Review the retail packaging, instructions and warranty against NOM-024. Agree who will correct Spanish-language consumer information or other commercial-information gaps before dispatch.
- Treat courier and low-value shipments as in scope until the Mexican customs representative confirms a different legal basis. The specific rule exemptions cited above do not cover the three smartphone lines.
- Freeze one clearance data sheet containing the model identifier, detailed commercial description, quantity, tariff line, invoice reference, packing-list reference and NOM evidence. Use the same identifiers across the exporter, forwarder, importer and customs representative.
- Keep CPTPP origin support separate from NOM compliance. Mexico's CPTPP customs resolution allows preferential treatment only for originating goods that satisfy the treaty and are supported by a valid origin certification; an origin claim does not replace product-safety or commercial-information evidence.

## Freight and import-export impact for Vietnam

The immediate freight risk is accepting cargo or fixing a delivery promise before the Mexican compliance file is ready. A mismatch between the model, tariff line, packaging and evidence can require clearance or delivery replanning after the shipment has already moved. None of the official sources cited here publishes a standard delay, detention amount, storage charge or inspection probability, so this guide makes no such estimate.

For Vietnamese exporters, the useful control point is therefore before booking confirmation: send the technical sheet and retail-packaging artwork to the importer, obtain written confirmation of the tariff line and NOM route, and only then align cargo-ready date and transport plan.

Confidence is high for the publication date, 1 July applicability date, three tariff lines and the stated scope of NOM-001, NOM-024 and the two rule exemptions. Shipment-level treatment remains dependent on the exact model, NICO, import regime and current instructions from the competent Mexican authorities. This article is operational guidance, not a customs ruling or legal opinion.
