This is a forecast, not a confirmed capacity, rate or shipment-volume outcome. Our base case for late 2026 into 2027 is that Vietnam's international logistics demand remains active but becomes more uneven by commodity and mode. Electronics, machinery and time-sensitive production inputs can keep air cargo and specialized services comparatively firm, while broad container demand may follow a flatter path.

The evidence does not justify calling a general freight boom. It supports a two-speed planning assumption.

## What the official evidence says

On 8 October, the World Trade Organization raised its forecast for world merchandise trade volume growth to 3.9% in 2026 and 4.1% in 2027. Asia is expected to record the fastest merchandise export and import growth in 2026, at 9.9% and 9.5% respectively. Yet the drivers are highly concentrated: AI-enabling goods accounted for 47% of global merchandise trade growth in value terms in the first half of 2026, and their trade value rose 67% year on year.

The WTO also reports a large value-volume gap. World merchandise trade value increased 15% in the first half of 2026, while volume rose 3.5%. Higher fuel and data-centre component prices explain part of the difference. A rising invoice value therefore does not automatically mean the same rise in tonnes, containers or shipments.

The WTO Goods Trade Barometer shows the same unevenness in physical indicators. Its July 2026 overall reading was 102.0 against a trend baseline of 100. Electronic components were at 104.9 and international air freight at 102.8, while the container shipping component was slightly below trend at 99.6. Export orders were at 103.5. These are momentum indicators relative to trend, not percentage growth rates and not forecasts for a specific Vietnam lane.

Vietnam's Ministry of Industry and Trade reported that exports increased 24.5% and imports 36.7% in the first nine months of 2026. Growth was concentrated in electronics, machinery and equipment. About 90.5% of imports were machinery, equipment, raw materials and fuels serving domestic production and export processing. Foreign-invested enterprises accounted for 80.6% of exports. This mix strengthens the production-chain signal, but it also makes the outlook dependent on a concentrated set of sectors and companies.

## Base, upside and downside cases

### Base case: active but split demand

Through early 2027, electronics, high-value components and production inputs are likely to support urgent, controlled and sometimes airfreight-compatible flows more strongly than broad container demand. Container volumes can still grow, but the WTO's latest mode indicators do not support assuming that every trade-value gain becomes proportional TEU growth.

### Upside case: AI investment stays strong

The WTO expects AI-related investment to remain an important trade driver through 2027. If confirmed orders, production schedules and physical component movements continue to expand, Vietnam's electronics supply chain could require tighter uplift, cut-off, handling and inventory coordination. This scenario must be confirmed by bookings and purchase orders, not inferred from a global percentage.

### Downside case: concentration and disruption bite

The WTO says the 2027 outlook depends on a timely resolution of the Middle East conflict and identifies a slowdown or reversal in AI investment as a significant risk. Vietnam also faces concentration risk because electronics, machinery and foreign-invested production account for much of the recent growth. In this case, trade value could remain elevated while units or general container cargo soften, and transport costs could stay volatile.

## Six controls for Vietnam freight planning

### 1. Separate value from physical cargo

Track invoice value, units, gross weight, chargeable weight, cubic metres, TEU and shipment count separately. Do not turn a 24.5% export-value increase or a global 4.1% trade-volume forecast into a lane capacity assumption.

### 2. Segment by commodity and service need

Keep electronics, machinery, production inputs, consumer goods, agricultural cargo and temperature-controlled goods in separate forecasts. A high-value component flow can move differently from a dense or low-value containerized product.

### 3. Use confirmed demand gates

Move an upside scenario into the operating plan only when purchase orders, production schedules, ready dates, booking requests and supplier confirmations support it. Record cancellations and delayed orders as quickly as new demand.

### 4. Plan air and ocean independently

Maintain separate capacity, cut-off, consolidation, dangerous-goods, security and contingency checks for air and ocean. The WTO barometer shows different momentum by mode; it does not provide a conversion ratio between them.

### 5. Watch the concentration risk

Review dependence on a few customers, foreign-invested manufacturers, electronics programmes, origins, destinations and gateways. A strong headline can hide weakness outside the leading segment.

### 6. Refresh the forecast with physical evidence

Update the plan when actual weight, TEU, uplift, rollovers, dwell time, cancellations and production-input arrivals differ from the scenario. Treat rates, space and transit times as quotation- and lane-specific.

## What this means for Vietnam import-export

The planning message is constructive but conditional: Vietnam can remain one of Asia's active production and trade nodes into 2027, while logistics demand becomes more specialized and less uniform. Shippers should budget and book from the commodity, service level and confirmed order file—not from trade value alone.

Confidence is moderate in the two-speed direction because the WTO outlook, physical barometer components and Vietnam's official trade mix point the same way. Confidence is low for any specific lane, monthly volume, rate, capacity shortage, modal share or delivery time because the official sources do not provide those outcomes. No guaranteed TEU growth, air-tonnage growth, rate movement, space availability, congestion, customs result or shipment outcome is claimed.
