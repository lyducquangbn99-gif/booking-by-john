This is an evidence-based forecast prepared on 11 November 2026. It is not a confirmed rate, capacity, uplift or transit-time notice.

Our base case for late 2026 through the first quarter of 2027 is that Vietnam's high-value and time-sensitive air cargo remains active, supported by electronics and production-input flows. The pressure will not be uniform: Asia-North America currently has the strongest major-corridor momentum, intra-Asia is firm, Europe-Asia is positive, and Middle East-Asia remains weaker. Shippers should therefore plan by cargo and lane rather than apply one global growth rate.

## What the newest official evidence says

IATA's September data show global cargo tonne-kilometres up 7.7% year on year while available cargo tonne-kilometres rose 2.7%. The global cargo load factor reached 47.9%, 2.2 percentage points higher than a year earlier. For Asia-Pacific airlines, demand rose 8.6%, capacity 4.9%, and the load factor reached 50.7%.

The corridor split is more important than the global average. September traffic was up 17.0% on Asia-North America, 10.2% within Asia and 6.0% on Europe-Asia. Middle East-Asia traffic fell 10.2%. These are global corridor results, not Vietnam-specific lane statistics, but they show why one booking assumption is unsafe.

Cost is a separate risk. IATA reported September jet-fuel prices 103.4% higher than a year earlier and said yields had not kept pace. That does not predict a particular Vietnam airfreight rate; it supports keeping fuel and carrier-cost exposure separate from the volume forecast.

Vietnam's latest National Statistics Office analysis gives a strong cargo-mix signal. In the first ten months of 2026, electronics, computers and components exports reached USD 149.41 billion, up 71.0%; imports reached USD 215.25 billion, up 74.4%. Production inputs represented 94.1% of goods-import value. These are value figures, not chargeable weight, aircraft capacity or shipment count.

The WTO expects world merchandise-trade volume to grow 4.1% in 2027 and says AI-related investment should remain an important driver. This supports continued activity, but it does not convert into a guaranteed Vietnam air-cargo growth rate.

## Base, upside and downside cases

### Base case: active demand, different lane pressures

Electronics, machinery and urgent production inputs keep Vietnam air cargo active into early 2027. Asia-North America and intra-Asia require the tightest booking discipline. Europe-Asia stays constructive. Middle East-linked routings need more schedule and connection verification even though their recent traffic is weaker.

### Upside case: confirmed technology orders extend the cycle

If AI-related investment becomes purchase orders, production schedules and physical component movements, high-value export and inbound-component demand could remain firm. Capacity should be committed only when shipment-level evidence confirms that scenario.

### Downside case: orders soften while costs remain high

An order slowdown, route disruption or persistent fuel pressure could reduce traffic without producing equally fast cost relief. A weak corridor can still be operationally difficult when connections or available capacity change.

## Six controls for Vietnam importers and exporters

### 1. Forecast each corridor separately

Maintain independent plans for Asia-North America, intra-Asia, Europe-Asia and Middle East-linked routings. Do not turn an IATA global or regional percentage into a Vietnam lane commitment.

### 2. Convert trade value into physical demand

Track pieces, gross weight, chargeable weight, dimensions, dangerous-goods status and ready date. USD growth is not a capacity forecast.

### 3. Link inbound components to outbound orders

Reconcile component arrivals with production slots and finished-goods deadlines. This reduces unnecessary expedites and prevents high import value from being mistaken for confirmed export cargo.

### 4. Use confirmation gates before buying space

Require a purchase order, supplier confirmation, cargo-ready date, packing data and consignee acceptance before moving optional capacity into the committed plan.

### 5. Separate volume, rate and fuel scenarios

Build different assumptions for cargo demand, available uplift, base rate and fuel-related charges. Verify the actual quotation and carrier notice for every booking.

### 6. Refresh weekly with operating evidence

Use actual bookings, accepted weight, offloads, connection failures, cancellations and lead times to replace the macro scenario. Keep a routing alternative for shipments whose delivery window cannot absorb a missed connection.

## What this forecast does not claim

It does not predict a universal air-cargo peak, a specific Vietnam tonnage increase, a capacity shortage, a rate change or a guaranteed transit time. It does not say every electronics shipment should move by air. The corridor figures cover the global scheduled-air-cargo market and cannot be assigned directly to one origin, airport, airline or product.

Confidence is moderate in the direction of active but lane-divergent demand because IATA's latest physical traffic data, Vietnam's electronics-heavy trade mix and the WTO outlook point the same way. Confidence is low for any specific rate, capacity, route or monthly shipment outcome until it is confirmed by current bookings, quotations and operating notices.
