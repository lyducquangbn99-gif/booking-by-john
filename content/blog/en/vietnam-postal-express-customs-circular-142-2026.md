Vietnam's Ministry of Finance issued Circular 142/2026/TT-BTC on 29 September 2026. It takes effect on 16 November 2026. These are separate dates: issuance and legal effect. As of this article's verification date, the circular has been issued but is not yet effective.

The circular amends customs procedures for export, import and transit goods sent through a designated postal operator or an international express service. It changes parts of Circular 49/2015/TT-BTC and Circular 191/2015/TT-BTC, together with later amendments, and replaces several forms and annexes.

## What changes in the shipment file

For postal imports, the revised Group 1 covers parcels within the import-duty exemption threshold referenced by the circular when they are also exempt from permits, conditions and specialized inspection, or are not subject to those controls. For express imports, the revised Group 2 uses the same two-part logic: the value condition alone is not enough.

The circular also connects these simplified groups to electronic identity and authentication. A postal operator declaring Group 1 cargo must comply with electronic-identification rules. An express operator declaring Group 1 or Group 2 cargo must do the same. When the operator acts for the cargo owner, it must notify the exporter or importer to coordinate with Customs on electronic identification and authentication.

Electronic filing remains central. Customs-dossier documents and tax documents are submitted, confirmed and used under the referenced electronic customs framework. If a declaration is routed to lane 3, the cargo must be presented for Customs inspection. The instrument also updates data fields, tax-payment processes, receipts and supporting forms.

## Six checks before 16 November

### 1. Identify the service channel

Record whether the consignment moves through the designated postal operator or an international express company. Circular 142 amends two different procedural circulars; do not copy one channel's group number, form or operator responsibility into the other without checking the applicable text.

### 2. Test value and regulatory controls separately

For the simplified import group, confirm both the customs-value condition and the permit, condition and specialized-inspection status. Do not label a parcel Group 1 or Group 2 only because it is low value. The circular refers to the exemption threshold in other legislation; it does not create a universal tax-free rule for every parcel.

### 3. Align electronic identity before the operator declares

Confirm who is the exporter, importer or cargo owner, who will act as customs declarant, and what electronic identification or authentication step is required. If the postal or express operator acts on the customer's behalf, keep evidence that the customer received the coordination request.

### 4. Reconcile the electronic data set

Match sender and recipient, goods description, value, currency, quantity, tax data, control status and supporting documents before transmission. Circular 142 replaces annexes and forms, while Customs is tasked with building the electronic data-processing system and issuing data-message formats that meet the circular.

### 5. Prepare for lane 3 and transit scenarios

Keep the parcel and records available if Customs routes the declaration to lane 3 and requires physical presentation. For postal items collected abroad for sorting and onward international transit through Vietnam, and for goods moving between border gates, apply the transit procedures referenced in the circular rather than treating the movement as an ordinary domestic handoff.

### 6. Confirm payment, receipt and record ownership

Where the operator pays duties, penalties, customs fees or transit-goods charges on the cargo owner's behalf, reconcile the declaration, pre-funded deposit or guarantee arrangement, receipt and customer settlement. For express operators using receipts, the circular links issuance and management to the electronic-invoice and electronic-document framework.

## Vietnam freight and import-export impact

The immediate impact is a data-and-authority readiness check for cross-border parcels. Exporters, importers, e-commerce sellers, postal operators, express companies and customs agents should agree the correct channel, group, declarant, customer identity, control status, data set and payment record before the effective date. A low-value description by itself does not establish the simplified group, tax exemption or freedom from specialized controls.

Circular 142 does not promise automatic release, a green lane, a fixed clearance time, freedom from inspection or a cost saving. It does not state that every courier shipment is tax exempt. Confidence is high on the instrument number, issue date, effective date and the procedural changes described above because they were checked against the Government record and the complete signed text. Shipment-level confidence remains conditional on the exact value, goods, sender and recipient, licence and specialized-inspection status, customs routing, operator system and the formats issued by Customs. This is practical editorial guidance, not a customs ruling or legal opinion.
