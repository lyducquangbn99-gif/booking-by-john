Vietnam issued Decree 384/2026/NĐ-CP on 9 October 2026. It takes effect on 1 January 2027 and establishes a dedicated framework for determining Vietnamese origin for goods produced and circulated in Vietnam.

The issue date and effective date are different. More importantly, the scope is narrow: the decree does not determine origin for export/import origin certification, does not set labelling methods and does not regulate traceability. A product sold domestically and later exported still needs a separate origin assessment under Vietnam's export-origin law and the destination market's rules.

## What changes on 1 January 2027

Goods can qualify as wholly obtained in Vietnam under Article 5 or as non-wholly obtained under Article 6. For goods made with imported or origin-unknown materials, Article 6 provides two alternative tests:

- A change in tariff classification at the four-digit HS heading level between the non-originating materials and the finished good made in Vietnam.
- Vietnamese value content of at least 30% of the ex-works price.

The Ministry of Industry and Trade says the value-content test may use a direct or indirect formula during the financial year. Its implementation guidance also warns that simple operations—including straightforward packaging changes, simple packing, attaching or printing labels, and simple assembly—are not enough by themselves to create Vietnamese origin.

The regime is based on self-determination and post-checking. It does not create an application for a domestic certificate of origin or a related fee. But once a business states that a product has Vietnamese origin, it is responsible for the accuracy of that statement and must prove the criteria when a competent authority asks.

## Seven controls before making or carrying the claim

### 1. Define the product and the claim

Record the exact model, specification, production site and domestic sales channel. Do not treat “made in Vietnam” on a domestic label, a preferential export C/O and a non-preferential origin declaration for a foreign market as interchangeable documents.

### 2. Build an HS map at input and finished-good level

For the tariff-shift route, identify the HS heading of every non-originating or origin-unknown material and the four-digit heading of the finished product. A purchasing description or supplier marketing name is not an HS determination. Keep the classification basis and resolve material disputes before printing claims.

### 3. Choose and document one qualifying route

State whether the product is wholly obtained, meets the four-digit tariff change or reaches at least 30% Vietnamese value content. If using value content, record the selected direct or indirect formula, the ex-works price, material values, period and calculation assumptions. Do not switch methods merely to reach a preferred result without a consistent file.

### 4. Exclude simple processing from the origin logic

Map the actual production steps performed in Vietnam. Repacking imported goods, changing packaging, adding labels, splitting lots or carrying out simple assembly does not independently establish Vietnamese origin. The file should show the substantive transformation or value creation on which the claim relies.

### 5. Link procurement evidence to the production record

Keep input invoices, bills of materials, supplier origin evidence where available, inventory movements, production descriptions and cost records. Ministry guidance specifically identifies input invoices, material schedules, production-process descriptions and related documents as evidence that businesses should retain.

### 6. Keep domestic and export origin files separate but reconciled

For a product that is both sold in Vietnam and exported, maintain a domestic Decree 384 file and the applicable export-origin file. The same materials and manufacturing facts should not contradict each other, but passing the domestic 30% or HS4 test does not automatically establish preferential origin under an FTA.

### 7. Control labels, commercial documents and transition stock

Approve origin wording across labels, catalogues, quotations, invoices and packing instructions from one verified product record. Article 12 allows goods whose labels correctly showed Vietnamese origin under the law before 1 January 2027 to keep those labels until the goods expire. This is a transition rule, not permission to apply new unverified claims.

## Concrete impact on Vietnam freight and import-export operations

Manufacturers using imported components will need cleaner links between inbound procurement, HS classification, production records and outbound product claims. Freight forwarders and customs brokers should not invent origin from the loading place, shipper address or a domestic label; the product owner should supply the verified statement and the shipment-specific export evidence.

Operationally, a late mismatch can hold up label approval, commercial-document release or export-origin preparation. That is an editorial risk assessment, not a statutory clearance time. Decree 384 itself does not impose a freight surcharge, change an import duty rate, grant tariff preference or guarantee that a foreign customs authority will accept Vietnamese origin.

Confidence is high on the decree number, issue date, effective date, scope exclusions, HS4/VVC 30% alternatives, evidence responsibility and transition rule because they appear in official Government and Ministry sources. Shipment-level confidence is medium: the correct outcome depends on product facts, HS classification, valuation records, other applicable origin rules and the destination authority. This article is operational guidance, not legal advice, an origin ruling or a C/O decision.
