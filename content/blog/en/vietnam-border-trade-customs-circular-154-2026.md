Vietnam's Ministry of Finance issued Circular 154/2026/TT-BTC on 15 October 2026. It was published in Official Gazette No. 567 on 30 October and takes effect on 1 January 2027. The issue date, Gazette publication date and effective date are therefore separate.

The circular amends Circular 80/2019/TT-BTC, which guides customs procedures, tax, fees and charges for exports and imports under the border-trade framework in Decree 14/2018/ND-CP. Its most operational changes concern goods traded by border residents and merchants that aggregate imported goods purchased from those residents.

This is a specialised channel. It should not be read as a new procedure for every commercial truck, every land-border shipment or every importer in Vietnam.

## What changes for merchants aggregating border-resident goods?

A merchant aggregating imported goods that benefited from the border resident's duty-free allowance must follow the commercial-import procedure referenced by the circular. The merchant must state each resident declaration number in the bill-of-lading field of the customs declaration and attach the resident declarations to the electronic system. For a paper declaration, the declarations accompany the customs dossier.

The customs dossier must be filed no later than 30 days from the date the merchant purchased the goods. Declaration and registration take place at the Customs office of the border gate nearest the place of purchase.

The merchant may sell or transport the aggregated goods from the border-gate area or customs-controlled collection and inspection location into the domestic market only after completing customs procedures and fully paying tax and other required amounts. The completed customs declaration can serve as the circulation document for inland transport.

The aggregated goods must be fully assembled at a warehouse, yard or central customs-controlled collection, inspection and supervision location within the border-gate area. The location must be arranged so it does not congest cargo or vehicles.

Where the original border-resident transaction has already fulfilled tax, fee, product-policy and quality-inspection obligations, the merchant does not repeat customs procedures for the aggregated goods. Customs determines that status from dossiers, documents, electronic data or information supplied by a competent authority. If the goods then circulate inland, the merchant must issue an electronic invoice under the applicable law.

## Electronic declarations and the outage fallback

Border residents must declare the information set in Appendix I through Customs' electronic data-processing system and are responsible for the completeness and authenticity of the data. The fields include identity and address, goods description, origin, unit, quantity, unit price, value and the number of import or export movements during the month. Customs records classification, value and applicable taxes.

If the system is unavailable or not ready, the border-gate Customs office guides the resident to use the paper forms in Appendix II. When the system returns, the customs officer must update the paper declaration information and the clearance or release decision in the system.

Goods above the duty-free allowance remain subject to declaration, tax and applicable product rules. If the import data are insufficient, Customs physically inspects the goods to determine their name, type and quantity for tax and other collections. Imported goods above the allowance must be physically inspected under the amended procedure.

At a border gate where neither the State Treasury nor a commercial bank operates a collection point, Customs may collect tax and other state-budget amounts in cash or by electronic payment such as a banking app, e-wallet or bank card, issue a receipt and transfer the money to the state budget under the referenced rules.

## Six checks before 1 January 2027

- Confirm that the flow is genuinely within the border-resident trade and merchant-aggregation channel; do not apply this checklist automatically to ordinary commercial cargo.
- Link every resident purchase to its declaration number, purchase date, seller identity and goods line; set a control well before the 30-day dossier deadline.
- Reconcile goods name, origin, unit, quantity, unit price, value and monthly movement count across the resident declaration, merchant customs declaration, invoice and stock record.
- Confirm the correct border-gate Customs office and approved customs-controlled collection location before moving or combining the goods.
- Block inland sale or transport until customs completion, taxes and other required amounts are confirmed; retain the completed declaration as the circulation document.
- Maintain an outage pack using the new paper forms, with a named owner for later system updating and evidence of the clearance or release decision.

## Vietnam freight and import-export impact

The practical freight effect is a tighter chain of evidence between the resident transaction, the merchant's consolidated customs file, tax status, electronic invoice and inland movement. A missing declaration number, an inconsistent goods line or late filing can interrupt consolidation and prevent lawful movement from the border area.

The circular also creates a clearer paper fallback and permits additional electronic payment methods where normal collection points are absent. It does not publish a standard clearance time, freight capacity, free-storage period, service fee or guaranteed cost saving. It does not remove product-policy, quarantine, food-safety, quality or other specialised-control requirements.

Confidence is high on the instrument number, issue date, Gazette publication, effective date and the requirements described above because they are confirmed by the official Government record, Official Gazette and signed 15-page text. Confidence is medium on shipment-level effects because the official sources do not quantify processing time, inspection frequency, border-gate readiness, congestion or cost. This is practical editorial guidance, not a customs ruling or legal opinion.
