Forecast label: this is a Booking by John evidence-based planning forecast for Vietnam seafood and cold-chain logistics in Q4 2026. It is not an official export forecast, a freight-rate forecast or a booking guarantee.

Vietnam entered Q4 with an active seafood production base. The National Statistics Office reported 7.8909 million tonnes of fisheries output in January-September, up 3.9% year on year. Aquaculture reached 4.8606 million tonnes, up 5.9%, while capture fisheries reached 3.0303 million tonnes, up 0.9%.

The official 2026 production plan is about 9.88 million tonnes. That leaves 1.9891 million tonnes for Q4, or about 663,000 tonnes per month. This required pace is 30.2% below the Q3 monthly average of 950,600 tonnes. The arithmetic suggests the annual production plan is reachable if output remains broadly resilient, but it does not show how much product will be export-ready or move in refrigerated containers.

## Export signal: positive, but less uniform

Seafood exports were estimated at USD 9.1 billion for January-September, up 11.5% year on year. September exports were about USD 1.1 billion, up 12.0%, and Q3 exports were nearly USD 3.4 billion, up 9.4%.

The official review says demand for pangasius was relatively favourable in China, the EU, CPTPP markets, Japan and the United Kingdom. It also says overall export growth had slowed in recent months and exports to the United States were trending sharply lower. That combination supports an active Q4 cold-chain workload, but not a uniform uplift across destinations.

## Three Q4 logistics scenarios

### Base case: active volume, uneven lanes

Confidence: medium. Aquaculture-led supply and positive export value keep processing, cold storage, reefer trucking and international booking work active. Market mix changes by species and destination, so booking plans need order-level evidence instead of a single national growth assumption.

### Upside case: stronger demand converts into confirmed shipments

Confidence: low to medium. Favourable demand in several markets turns into firm purchase orders, export-compliant lots and confirmed sailings. More cargo moves without a broad capacity problem. This depends on buyer demand, product specification, origin evidence, food-safety acceptance and live carrier capacity.

### Downside case: production grows faster than export conversion

Confidence: low to medium. Output remains available, but weaker demand in one or more destinations, compliance gaps or shipment exceptions slow the conversion of product into export cargo. Cold-store dwell and replanning work may rise for affected lots, but the official sources do not quantify that outcome.

## Six controls for the next booking cycle

- Link every booking to a confirmed order, species, product form, lot, processing date, destination and required delivery window.
- Reconcile farm or vessel origin, traceability records, health or food-safety documents and buyer specifications before shipping instructions close.
- Record cargo temperature, pre-cooling status, reefer set point, ventilation and any humidity requirement at handoff.
- Confirm empty-equipment condition, pre-trip inspection, plug availability, cut-offs and contingency contacts with the responsible depot, warehouse and carrier.
- Separate destination demand. Do not use national export growth as proof that the United States, EU, China, Japan or another lane has the same order or capacity outlook.
- Review exceptions weekly: unconfirmed orders, incomplete certificates, temperature deviations, missed cut-offs, rejected lots and revised arrival requirements.

## Freight and import-export impact

The practical forecast is continued cold-chain activity with more variation by product and market. Aquaculture is growing faster than capture fisheries, so exporters and forwarders should expect origin, processing and traceability work to matter as much as vessel space. Positive export value is a commercial signal, not evidence of a specific TEU volume, reefer shortage, rate increase, port delay or guaranteed shipment.

Confidence is high for the official production and export estimates and for the transparent planning arithmetic. Confidence is medium for the base-case direction. Confidence is low for any species, buyer, lane, carrier, equipment position, rate or shipment until current operational evidence is checked.
