Vietnam exporters booking Mediterranean cargo with Maersk now have a dated cost checkpoint. On 4 November 2026, the carrier announced a Peak Season Surcharge (PSS) and a separate Heavy Load Surcharge (HWS), both using an effective Price Calculation Date of 23 November 2026 for the Far East Asia–Mediterranean trade.

Vietnam is expressly included in the Far East Asia origin list. The Mediterranean destination list includes Italy, Spain, France, Portugal, Greece, Turkey, Egypt and other named markets, and the notices also cover other European destinations routed through a Mediterranean final discharge port.

The PSS is USD 750 for all 20-foot equipment and USD 1,500 for all 40- and 45-foot equipment. The HWS is USD 400 for all types of 20-foot equipment when Verified Gross Mass (VGM) is greater than 25 metric tonnes. Maersk says the HWS applies across its ocean products, including contract products, SPOT and Maersk Go.

For a 20-foot unit above 25 tonnes VGM that is within both notices, USD 750 plus USD 400 equals USD 1,150. That figure is an editorial calculation from the two announced tariffs, not a carrier quote or a universal invoice amount. Other freight, local, contingency and contractual charges may apply, and the carrier says surcharges are reviewed regularly.

## Six checks before confirming the shipment

### 1. Confirm that the routing is in scope

Check origin, final destination, equipment and the actual final discharge port. A shipment to another European country may still fall within the notice if its route uses a Mediterranean final discharge port. Do not decide scope from the consignee’s country alone; obtain the routing and written quotation.

### 2. Record the Price Calculation Date

For non-SPOT, non-FMC bookings, Maersk defines PCD in these notices as the scheduled departure date of the first water leg at the time of booking confirmation. The HWS notice says SPOT pricing is retrieved from the booking-confirmation date. Save the booking confirmation and quotation version, and ask the carrier to identify the PCD and each applicable surcharge.

### 3. Calculate exposure by equipment

Use the announced tariff as a screening control:

- all 20-foot equipment: PSS USD 750;
- all 40- and 45-foot equipment: PSS USD 1,500;
- 20-foot equipment with VGM greater than 25 tonnes: HWS USD 400 in addition to any applicable PSS.

The HWS wording says “greater than 25 metric tons”, not “25 tonnes or more”. Do not use the threshold as a reason to under-declare weight or operate without a safe margin.

### 4. Reconcile VGM before the carrier cut-off

Maersk defines VGM for this notice as the cargo weight, including dunnage and bracing, plus the container tare weight. Under IMO’s SOLAS framework, the shipper is responsible for providing verified weight in the shipping document sufficiently early for the stowage plan, and VGM is a condition for loading a packed container.

Reconcile the packing list, cargo weight, dunnage, securing material, tare plate and weighing record. If the expected total is near 25 tonnes, complete the approved weighing process early enough to resolve a discrepancy without missing cut-off.

### 5. Compare the quote with the sales contract

Identify who bears ocean-freight surcharges under the sales contract and agreed Incoterm. Ask whether the carrier quote includes the PSS and HWS, how later changes are handled, and whether any local or contingency charges remain outside the quoted total. A commercial allocation between seller and buyer does not change the shipper’s VGM safety responsibility.

### 6. Give finance and the customer a dated change log

Record the 4 November notice date, the 23 November PCD, the quote date, equipment, VGM and responsible owner. If the carrier revises or withdraws a tariff, retain the newer notice with the quotation. This is more reliable than using an undated surcharge assumption in a margin sheet.

## Concrete impact on Vietnam freight and exports

The immediate risk is not only a higher line item. A Vietnam exporter can price a Mediterranean order using one equipment plan, then discover that the confirmed PCD falls on or after 23 November or that a heavy 20-foot unit crosses the VGM threshold. That can create a margin gap, a buyer dispute or a late equipment discussion.

Add three fields to the pre-booking gate for this lane: `confirmed PCD`, `equipment and forecast VGM`, and `carrier surcharge breakdown`. For qualifying 20-foot cargo, show the USD 1,150 arithmetic separately from base freight and all other charges. For 40- and 45-foot cargo, show the USD 1,500 PSS but do not add the HWS from this particular notice.

Confidence is high in the announced rates, scope and date because both rate notices were published by Maersk on 4 November 2026, and the VGM responsibilities come from IMO. Uncertainty remains for any actual shipment: booking type, routing, contract terms, later tariff changes, local rules and the live quotation determine the charge. No source used here guarantees space, equipment, sailing, transit time, customs release, final price or surcharge duration.
