Vietnam now has a working reference point for electric inland container transport. Nike, CMA CGM and Gemadept announced that a fully electric river barge has entered service between Nike’s inland drop-off location in Ho Chi Minh City, in the former Binh Duong province, and the Gemalink deep-sea terminal at Cai Mep.

The partners describe a 180-kilometre round trip powered by batteries and certified renewable electricity from a solar farm at Gemalink. CMA CGM says the barge is designed to carry more than 50,000 TEUs annually and can recharge in about four hours. The two launch announcements say the operation produces zero direct greenhouse gas emissions on the route and is expected to avoid approximately 778 tonnes of CO₂ each year.

Those statements need careful boundaries. The barge is serving Nike cargo, not the whole market. “Zero direct emissions” refers to emissions during operation; it is not the same as zero lifecycle emissions from manufacturing the batteries, barge and charging system. The annual avoidance figure is an estimate published by the project partners, not an independently verified result from a full year of service.

## Six checks before using this model in an export plan

### 1. Confirm that the service is actually open to your cargo

Do not treat the launch as a public timetable or a general booking product. Ask the logistics provider whether your shipper, cargo, origin point, volume and destination terminal are eligible. Obtain a written service scope before quoting a lower-carbon option to a buyer.

### 2. Connect the barge plan to the ocean-vessel cut-off

An inland waterway movement only works if its loading window, sailing time, terminal handover and contingency buffer fit the mother-vessel cut-off at Gemalink. Record the responsible party for each handover and the latest recovery point if a barge connection is missed.

### 3. Keep an agreed road fallback

Battery charging, berth availability, waterway conditions, cargo readiness and vessel schedule changes can affect the chain. Define when cargo switches to road, who approves the switch and how the resulting cost and emissions are recorded. Resilience comes from an explicit exception path, not from assuming one mode will always operate perfectly.

### 4. Separate direct emissions from lifecycle claims

Use the exact boundary supplied by the service provider. “Zero direct greenhouse gas emissions” on the barge route should not be rewritten as “zero-emission export” or “carbon-neutral shipment.” Ask whether the comparison is against truck transport, a diesel barge or another baseline and whether the electricity certificate covers the relevant charging period.

### 5. Agree the evidence for each shipment

For a buyer’s Scope 3 file, define the shipment reference, container count, route, mode, electricity evidence, calculation method, baseline, reporting period and party responsible for the data. A yearly project estimate is not automatically a shipment-level certificate.

### 6. Price and measure the whole inland move

Compare the full service, including pickup, consolidation, terminal handling, barge transport, charging-related operating windows, exception trucking and administrative reporting. The official launch sources do not state a public rate, guaranteed saving, transit-time advantage or general capacity entitlement.

## What it means for Vietnam freight and export operations

The immediate importance is operational proof: a battery-electric barge, dedicated charging infrastructure and renewable electricity have been assembled into one live inland-to-deep-sea chain in southern Vietnam. For exporters facing buyer requests for lower transport emissions, it creates a concrete model to discuss with carriers, terminal operators and large customers.

It may also encourage more customer-specific waterway solutions between industrial cargo points and Cai Mep. But replication remains an ambition stated by the project partners, not a confirmed network expansion. Exporters should not infer that electric barge space is generally available, that road transport will disappear, or that ocean freight rates, customs treatment or vessel schedules have changed.

Confidence is high in the launch date, route, operating design and stated design figures because they come directly from CMA CGM, Nike and Gemalink. Confidence is moderate in the expected annual emissions benefit because it is a forward-looking partner estimate. No official source used here provides independently audited operating results, a public tariff, a service-level guarantee or access terms for other shippers.
