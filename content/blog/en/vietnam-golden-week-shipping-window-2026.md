The late-September planning window has become concrete for Vietnam exporters. China's official 2026 holiday calendar sets the National Day break from 1 to 7 October. Carrier notices now identify named blank sailings around that period, while a separate East Asia weather advisory says recurring weather is already contributing to congestion and weaker on-time vessel arrivals.

This is not evidence of a shutdown at every Asian port or a shortage on every Vietnam route. It is a prompt to check the actual carrier, service, voyage and connection behind each booking before document and cargo cut-offs close.

## What the official notices say

Maersk's 9 September Asia Pacific update advises customers with Q4 requirements to review schedules and space early and, where appropriate, consider late-September departures ahead of Golden Week. It also warns that seasonal weather and terminal congestion can affect one sailing and cascade into later voyages.

On the Transpacific network, Maersk lists an eastbound TP8 blank sailing with a pro forma first-port departure from Busan on 9 October and an eastbound TP12 blank sailing from Ningbo on 9 October. The notice says alternative inducement calls will maintain port coverage, but it does not promise that every original routing, cut-off or connection remains unchanged.

For Far East Asia–Europe, Maersk lists three blank sailings: AE15 from Qingdao on 28 September, AE12 from Ningbo on 8 October and AE1 from Shanghai on 10 October. The carrier says coverage plans are in place and bookings remain open through usual channels.

Hapag-Lloyd's official North America notice is directly relevant to northern Vietnam. It lists no US2 sailing from Ningbo on 7 October and says alternative coverage will be provided by the US1 service: Maersk Shams is scheduled to depart Haiphong on 2 October and make an additional call at Norfolk. That is a named alternative, not a blanket replacement for every Vietnam–US East Coast booking.

## Six checks before the window closes

### 1. Identify the service, not just the trade lane

Record carrier, service code, vessel, voyage, load port, transshipment port and final destination. A notice affecting TP8, TP12, AE15, AE12, AE1 or US2 should not automatically be applied to a different service.

### 2. Reconfirm the cut-off chain

Ask for the latest cargo cut-off, shipping-instruction cut-off, VGM cut-off and any dangerous-goods or reefer deadline. A sailing that remains available can still have a revised cut-off or connection.

### 3. Test the Haiphong 2 October option precisely

For US East Coast cargo, confirm whether the US1 voyage from Haiphong accepts the commodity and equipment, whether Norfolk is the final port or a connection point, and what inland or feeder leg follows. Keep the carrier's written routing confirmation with the booking file.

### 4. Hold a dated fallback

Compare at least one departure before and one after the preferred sailing. Record the last decision date, extra transshipment, equipment requirement and document changes for each option. Do not assume “port coverage” means the original transit plan is preserved.

### 5. Prioritise cargo by consequence

Separate production-critical, contract-dated, perishable and ordinary replenishment cargo. Give scarce planning attention to the shipments whose missed connection would have the largest operational consequence; consider air, rail or storage only after checking cargo suitability and total routing.

### 6. Run a daily exception check

Until departure, review carrier advisories and the booking status for vessel substitution, port omission, cut-off change and weather impact. Name one person who can approve a routing change and one deadline after which the shipment stays on the confirmed plan.

## Vietnam freight and export impact

The immediate effect is on sequencing: factories, truckers, customs brokers, forwarders and buyers need one shared booking version before late-September hand-offs. Northern exporters considering the named Haiphong sailing should verify acceptance and downstream routing rather than treating the additional Norfolk call as universal coverage.

Confidence is high in the official holiday dates and the named carrier schedule changes. Confidence is medium for any individual shipment until its live booking and schedule are reconfirmed, because carrier plans and weather can change. No standard freight rate, capacity shortage, transit delay, cost saving or guaranteed alternative is claimed.
